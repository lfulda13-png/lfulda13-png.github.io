Research

BA thesis · in progress

The Price of War: Regime Type and the Economic Cost of Conflict

In Perpetual Peace (1795), Kant makes a surprisingly fiscal argument: where citizens must consent to war, they will hesitate, because they would have to carry its costs themselves. My thesis asks whether this shows up in the data. Do the economic costs of war differ between democracies and autocracies?

The starting point is the new long-run dataset of Federle, Meier, Müller, Mutschler and Schularick, “The Price of War” (American Economic Review 116(3), 2026). I replicate their baseline from the public replication package and extend it by regime type.
